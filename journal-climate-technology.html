Beyond equipment: reading climate technology as a system

Climate technology is often reduced to equipment: a solar panel, a battery, a monitoring instrument or a treatment plant. That view is useful, but incomplete. Technologies operate through physical artefacts, knowledge, institutions, infrastructure, energy, materials and maintenance systems.

The pilot Climate Technology Atlas starts from the CTCN mitigation and adaptation taxonomy. It then uses the IIASA distinction between hardware, software and orgware to describe the technological system around each record. Complementary typologies help indicate flexibility, hard and soft components, technological form and environmental role.

The current version is exploratory. Raw-material and country information identifies global production and processing contexts, not the traceable origin of a specific supplier. The atlas should therefore be read as an analytical and communication tool under review, not as a new official CTCN classification.
